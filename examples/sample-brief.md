*All times Pacific. Markets are closed for the weekend; no new session since Friday Sept 25's close (covered in Saturday's brief). This brief tracks weekend developments and is written for Monday's open.*

## 1. Top 3

- **Iran raised a new Hormuz offer; Trump answered with a map, not a signature.** Hours after Trump told reporters Saturday morning he'd rejected Iran's seven-day roadmap, Iran's delegation in New York floated a fuller reopening offer. Trump's response, posted to Truth Social overnight Friday into Saturday, was an image of the strait labeled "Trump Strait," with the White House offering no elaboration (Fortune, WITN, Boston Globe — Sept 26). Separately, Fortune reported the US military is now guiding tankers through the strait in daylight and has helped roughly double the oil volume exiting the Gulf versus the low reached during the campaign. Why it matters: Friday's oil selloff priced de-escalation that still hasn't been signed, and Monday's open inherits an unresolved, headline-driven standoff.
- **Brussels made the energy-crisis warning official and dated it to Mideast spillover.** The European Commission sent member states a letter (reported Bloomberg/Euronews, Sept 26–27) warning of a winter energy price crisis and urging gas-demand curbs — 2022-crisis-style measures like peak-hour cuts and public-building temperature limits, though nothing mandatory yet. EU gas storage is ~70% full, about 12 points behind this time last year. Dutch TTF is near €72/MWh, up roughly €40 since the US and Israel struck Iran on Feb 28, with analysts warning of spikes above €100/MWh if Gulf LNG exports don't grow or Norwegian maintenance drags on. Why it matters: this moves the Hormuz story from a US oil-price headline into a European inflation and growth risk with an explicit institutional warning behind it.
- **Bloomberg named bond-market volatility itself as the next credit signal to watch.** The MOVE index — Treasury volatility's equivalent of VIX — hit roughly 105 this week, the highest since March and well above its ~80 ten-year norm. Bloomberg's credit-weekly column (Sept 26) frames that jump as a lead indicator for corporate-spread widening, comparable to early 2023. It hasn't shown up in spreads yet: IG was ~77bp Thursday, about 2bp tighter than the start of the month, and JPMorgan strategists call current high-grade spreads roughly 7bp too tight for the risk. Why it matters: this is the same divergence flagged in Saturday's brief (Oracle-specific credit stress vs. flat broad spreads) but now with a named, rates-side leading indicator pointing the same way.
