Today is the date shown by running `date`. Check it first; every freshness rule below depends on it.

## Task
Write a 3–5 minute morning markets brief and save it to `briefs/YYYY-MM-DD.md`.
If yesterday's file exists in `briefs/`, read it and report day-over-day changes in the Dashboard.

All outputs from this task — the markdown brief, any HTML dashboard artifact, any reference/guideline pages — get a local copy saved into `briefs/`, alongside the markdown file, using the same `YYYY-MM-DD` date prefix (e.g. `briefs/YYYY-MM-DD-dashboard.html`). Nothing generated by this task is left only in the scratchpad or only as a hosted artifact link.

## Searches (one query per topic, use web search)
1. Asia and Europe market close; US stock futures
2. Treasury yields 2-year, 10-year, 30-year; curve moves
3. Treasury auction results (last 2 days)
4. Fed speakers and rate-hike/cut odds (FedWatch)
5. ECB, BoJ, BoE news
6. Economic data released yesterday vs consensus; today's economic calendar
7. Oil prices and geopolitical drivers
8. Gold and copper
9. Dollar index, USD/JPY, emerging-market FX stress
10. High-yield and investment-grade credit spreads; private credit / BDC news
11. VIX and funding/repo stress

13. The single biggest market story this morning (catch-all)

Paywalled articles (Bloomberg, WSJ, FT) usually can't be fetched. Use their headlines as signals only, and confirm facts from free sources (CNBC, AP, Reuters, Trading Economics, treasury.gov, federalreserve.gov, FRED).

### Reproducible headline pull (run before the searches above)
`python3 briefs/scripts/fetch_news.py <YYYY-MM-DD>` — one concurrent pass over **112 public RSS feeds**, no API key, in three tiers:

1. **Topic desks** — 26 topics × 3 Google News queries (78 queries): the brief's original 13 research topics plus global-macro regions (China, Japan, Europe, UK, EM) and the playbook's structural themes (private credit, AI capex financing, market structure, trade/tariffs, energy & power, banks, labor).
2. **Publisher feeds** — 27 direct newsroom and primary-institution feeds: CNBC ×5, MarketWatch ×3, Investing.com ×4, Yahoo Finance, OilPrice.com; and the institutions themselves — Federal Reserve ×4 (press, monetary, speeches, testimony), ECB, BoE, BoJ, Treasury, BLS, Census, EIA ×2.
3. **Premium desks** — Google News `site:` queries against the paywalled outlets this tool stands in for: FT, WSJ, The Economist, Reuters, Bloomberg, Barron's, Nikkei Asia, IMF/BIS/OECD. Headlines only; per the Rules below these are signals to confirm from a free source, never citable facts on their own.

One network pass, two windows: the weekly pull is filtered down to the daily one, so nothing is fetched twice.

- `briefs/data/<date>-news.json` — daily window (36h, matches the freshness rule below)
- `briefs/data/<date>-news-weekly.json` — weekly window (168h), plus per-day buckets, top-source and topic-volume rollups

Quality filtering happens at ingest (`is_noise`): single-name corporate-action boilerplate (dividend declarations, 52-week prints, options-flow notes, coverage initiations), first-person advice columns, and section-index pages are dropped, since broad newsroom feeds carry a lot of it and it crowds out macro news. Outlet suffixes are stripped from titles (`strip_outlet_suffix`) and summaries that merely restate the headline are discarded (`is_redundant_summary`). Undated items are kept rather than dropped — Google News omits `pubDate` on some entries, and an unknown timestamp isn't evidence of staleness. A feed that fails is recorded in the output's `fetch_errors` ledger (and surfaced on the HTML page) rather than aborting the run, so a thin section is never silently mistaken for a quiet market.

Unit tests: `python3 -m pytest briefs/tests/test_fetch_news.py -q` (54 tests over the dedup keying, windowing, noise filter, outlet/summary transforms, age formatting, and RSS/Atom parsing — no network).

This is the reproducible starting point for the searches above, not a replacement for them: an RSS pull can't find named-analyst reaction, resolve a source disagreement, or read past a paywall. Triage it, then point the manual searches at what's worth chasing. Some topics will legitimately have nothing in the window — that's a real "Nothing notable," not a bug.

### News reader (HTML)
`python3 briefs/scripts/render_news.py <YYYY-MM-DD>` → `briefs/<date>-news.html`, built from the two JSON files above via `briefs/templates/news.html.j2`. A standalone reader meant to replace opening WSJ/FT directly:

- **Daily ⇄ Weekly toggle** — both windows live in one DOM (each story carries `is_daily`), so switching is a CSS class flip plus a recount, not a re-render or a second payload.
- **Sticky scroll-spy rail** — every section and topic is one click away from any scroll position, with live counts; the rail highlights whichever section owns the top of the viewport (resolved in document order, with an end-of-page case so the last sections can still activate).
- **Timestamps throughout** — relative age on every headline (`3h ago`), exact Pacific publication time on hover, freshness accent under 6 hours, and the pull time in the masthead.
- **Live filter** (`/` to focus) — matches title, source and summary; sections and groups that empty out hide themselves.
- **Front page** — newest stories across all tiers, deduped and capped at 2 per outlet so a high-frequency wire can't crowd out the desks worth reading first.
- **Week in review** — the weekly window bucketed by publication day, newest first.
- **Source ledger** — which outlets dominated, which topics carried volume, and any feed that failed this run.

Rendered with `autoescape=True` (unlike `render_dashboard.py`): headlines are untrusted third-party text, and only `http(s)` links are emitted as anchors.

## Full refresh — the whole thing, in order
Every artifact for a given day is reproduced by running these five commands. Nothing is hand-edited as HTML.

```bash
D=2026-09-25                                        # the date to build
python3 briefs/scripts/fetch_news.py      $D        # 112 RSS feeds  -> <D>-news.json, <D>-news-weekly.json
python3 briefs/scripts/fetch_market_data.py $D      # Yahoo + FRED   -> <D>-market-data.json, <D>-heatmap.csv
python3 briefs/scripts/merge_top_stories.py $D      # research draft -> folds into <D>-narrative.json
python3 briefs/scripts/render_news.py     $D        #                -> briefs/<D>-news.html
python3 briefs/scripts/render_dashboard.py $D       #                -> briefs/<D>-dashboard.html
```

The only step that isn't a data pull is the narrative: `<D>-narrative.json` (panel copy, "Read:" lines, playbook
tags) and `<D>-top-stories-draft.json` (the researched stories) are judgment, written from that day's reporting
per the Searches and Signal-preservation rules below. `merge_top_stories.py` folds the draft into the narrative
and reports what it merged, including any quote whose date could not be established — those render as "date not
established" rather than carrying an invented timestamp.

Both HTML pages share one analysis layer, `briefs/scripts/lib_news_digest.py`, so they agree by construction
rather than by being edited in step. It computes three readings from the headline corpus, none of which is a
claim about markets — they are claims about *coverage*:

- **Attention shift** — each topic's volume today against the **median** of its own prior days. Median, and prior
  days only, because a full-window mean includes today (inflating its own baseline) and spans a weekend (whose
  near-zero counts make every weekday read as a surge).
- **Corroboration** — headlines clustered by shared vocabulary, then counted by *distinct outlet*: the brief's
  "count independent sources, not articles" rule made mechanical. A floor, not proof — syndication means several
  outlets can still be one wire, and the rendered panel says so.
- **Playbook exposure** — topic volume mapped onto the playbook's six structural themes, so the frame gets a
  measured read rather than an asserted one.

`fetch_news.py` and `render_news.py` are documented under "Reproducible headline pull" and "News reader" above.

## Dashboard pipeline (fully data-driven — `briefs/templates/dashboard.html.j2` has no hardcoded day-specific content)
The HTML dashboard is assembled by code from three inputs, never hand-edited as HTML:
1. `python3 briefs/scripts/fetch_market_data.py <YYYY-MM-DD>` (installs `yfinance`/`pandas` if missing) — pulls ~30 sector/factor/asset-class ETFs from Yahoo Finance (1D/1W/1M/3M/YTD returns), 8 macro series from FRED (yields, HY/IG OAS, dollar index, VIX), a duration-bucketed Treasury/credit ETF matrix joined with static effective-duration metadata (`fi_matrix` — SHY/IEI/IEF/TLT/LQD/HYG/EMB/MUB; duration figures are approximate, per issuer fact sheets, since no free API exposes fund-level duration), and sector-SPDR relative-valuation multiples (`valuation` — trailing/forward P/E, P/B, div yield, each sector's peer-z-score and premium/discount to SPY). It also pulls two full-history FRED cross-sections, each sampled at six lookbacks (Now, 1W/1M/3M/1Y/3Y ago — `CURVE_SNAPSHOT_OFFSETS`), not just today: `yield_curve` (all 11 Treasury tenors, 1M through 30Y) and `credit_curve` (7-rung AAA→CCC OAS, in bp). These feed the yield-curve-evolution and credit-curve-evolution overlay charts — how the *whole* curve has moved, not just a couple of points on it. Writes `briefs/data/<date>-market-data.json` and `briefs/data/<date>-heatmap.csv`.
2. Write `briefs/data/<date>-narrative.json` by hand, from that day's research (`briefs/data/<date>-news.json` as the reproducible headline layer, triaged and deepened by the Searches above + the Signal-preservation rules below). This is the one step that can't be scripted — it requires reading and judging the news. Structure: `meta`, `top_stories`, `top_extra`, `dashboard_table`, `heatmap_note`, `valuation_note`, `equities_panel`, `fixed_income_panel`, `duration_note`, `macro_note`, `central_banks`, `cb_matrix` (policy rate / real rate / CPI YoY / growth proxy / stance per major bank — feeds the CB-rates quadrant chart plus the real-rate and carry-vs-Fed comparison bars; source the CPI/growth inputs same as any other narrative number, don't leave them as placeholders), `credit_section`, `stagflation` (or whatever the day's live debate is), `playbook`, `watchlist`, `sources`. Use the most recent `<date>-narrative.json` in `briefs/data/` as the schema reference — copy its shape, replace its content.
3. `python3 briefs/scripts/render_dashboard.py <YYYY-MM-DD>` — loads all three files, renders the heatmap `<tbody>`, macro-panel tiles, valuation bars, duration-matrix rows, today's-snapshot credit-curve bars, the color-coded yield/credit **change matrices** (`build_yield_change_matrix_html` / `build_credit_change_matrix_html` — Δ in bp vs. each lookback, red/green by direction, hover for the raw from→to values), the per-tenor/per-rating **deep-dive** `<details>` blocks (`build_yield_deep_dive_html` / `build_credit_deep_dive_html` — click to expand the full 6-snapshot value trail, no JS), and the **Market Pulse** movers-by-horizon scoreboard (`build_market_pulse_html`, from the same heatmap CSV — top/bottom 5 per period, "see top 10" `<details>`) as HTML (`lib_heatmap.py`); computes the yield-curve, CB-rates-quadrant, yield-curve-evolution, and credit-curve-evolution SVG geometry from raw values, the real-rate/carry-vs-Fed comparison-bar geometry from `cb_matrix`, and the **News Pulse** week/month story log (`build_news_pulse` — scans every `<date>-narrative.json` in `briefs/data/` and buckets each day's `top_stories`/`top_extra` into "this week" / "this month," so nothing is ever retyped: it just accumulates as more daily briefs get written) (all in `render_dashboard.py` — the yield/credit-curve-evolution charts share one geometry function, `compute_evolution_geometry`); and renders `templates/dashboard.html.j2` (Jinja2, via the reusable `evolution_chart` macro) into `briefs/<date>-dashboard.html`. Open that file directly (or drag it into a browser) — it isn't published as a hosted Artifact.

This means: rerunning steps 1 and 3 alone (same narrative.json) refreshes only the numbers — true one-command reproducibility for the quantitative side, including the full curve-evolution/change-matrix history (no manual snapshotting required; FRED's public history endpoint is queried once per series and sliced locally) and the News/Market Pulse rollups (built entirely from files already on disk — no narrative fields to author for them). A full refresh with new news requires step 2 in between, because narrative synthesis is a judgment call, not a data pull — that's also why the Signal-preservation rules exist, to keep that step's output attributable rather than flattened. Sanity-check FRED/Yahoo numbers against news-reported ones in the narrative (note any mismatch rather than silently picking one, same as any other source disagreement).

Also fully data-driven, no narrative authoring required:
- **Global macro matrix** (`fetch_global_macro_matrix` / `GLOBAL_MACRO_COUNTRIES` — 7 countries × policy rate, CPI YoY, output QoQ, consumption QoQ, each at 1M–3Y lookbacks from FRED's OECD-sourced series) — rendered as four cross-country change/trajectory matrices in the `#globalmacro` section, the same "how far has this moved" read as the yield/credit matrices but across countries instead of tenors/ratings.
- **Streamgraphs** — two, each built from a real walked time series (everything else quantitative in this pipeline is point-in-time snapshots or a handful of discrete lookbacks): a **sector-rotation streamgraph** (`build_sector_streamgraph`, monthly-resampled, indexed-to-100 sector-SPDR prices over the trailing year, reusing the same Yahoo Finance pull the heatmap already makes — no extra network call) in `#heatmap`, and a **credit-curve streamgraph** (the `stream` key `fetch_credit_curve_history` now also returns — full monthly OAS history by rating, not just the 6 snapshots) in `#duration`. Both share one geometry function, `compute_streamgraph_geometry` in `render_dashboard.py` — a from-scratch silhouette-offset (baseline centered at `-total(t)/2`) stacked-area computation, same "compute pixel geometry in Python, template only draws it" pattern as the yield curve/CB quadrant/evolution charts. Layer colors are generated by rotating hue evenly across the layer count (`hsl(i·360/n, 52%, 52%)`), not hand-picked, so it stays reproducible for any number of layers.

The dashboard's interactivity (line-toggle checkboxes on the curve-shape overlays, click-to-expand `<details>` on every deep-dive, the sticky section nav) is plain HTML/CSS/vanilla JS baked into `dashboard.html.j2` — no build step, no framework, works from a local file opened directly in a browser, and is identical every day since it's part of the template, not the narrative.

### Dashboard sections driven by the news pull
When `<date>-news.json` exists, `render_dashboard.py` adds two sections via `lib_news_digest.py` (and silently omits them when it doesn't, so the dashboard still builds without a news pull):
- **Newsroom** — the newest stories across every tier, deduped and capped at 2 per outlet, each with its publication time; plus what each paywalled desk is leading with.
- **News analysis** — the three computed readings above, plus "argument pieces worth reading" (headlines whose wording signals a claim rather than a report, weighted toward premium desks and the last 12 hours). Surfaced for reading; nothing there is summarized into a fact.

### Charts: what each form is for, and what was rejected
- **Top stories** carry the underlying mechanics, a dated timeline, named quotes with the date each was said, a sources list with publication dates, and an explicit `disagreement` field when sources conflict. Timestamps are mandatory: an undated quote renders as "date not established" rather than being dropped or guessed at.
- **Sector leadership** is a rank (bump) chart on trailing 3-month return, *replacing a stacked streamgraph*. The streamgraph was the wrong form: every sector was indexed to 100 at the window start, so the bands were near-identical widths and the chart read as a flat rainbow slab — the one thing it made visually obvious was the one thing that barely varied. Rotation is an ordering phenomenon, so rank is the honest encoding and a line crossing another *is* a leadership change. Ranked on a rolling return rather than cumulative-since-start, which would be dominated by whichever sector led in month one.
- **Credit spreads by rating** are log-scaled lines, *replacing the credit streamgraph*. Log because the rungs span two orders of magnitude (AAA ~40bp vs CCC ~1,100bp) and a linear axis flattens all of investment grade onto the floor. Stacking was also wrong: these rungs are alternatives, not components, so a stacked total had no meaning.
- **The yield curve** draws today's curve over the same tenors three months ago (dashed, from the FRED curve history), with a per-tenor bp delta readout. Today's curve alone says where rates are; the pair says what the move was.
- **Global macro trend view** stops each line at that series' **last real observation** and marks actual prints with dots. Previously every line was forward-filled to today, which drew a long flat line that read as "inflation was constant" when it actually meant "nobody has published since" — several of these OECD series lag by a year or more. Each country's data vintage is shown in the legend, red when more than four months stale.

## Rules
- Drop any item not dated within the last 36 hours.
- No number without a source and timestamp. If sources disagree, show both.
- Count independent sources, not articles. Several outlets rewriting one wire story is one source.
- Keep what the reporting says moved markets separate from your own interpretation. Label interpretation as "Read:".
- Split a rates move into real yield, breakeven, and term premium only when you have data for it. Otherwise say it's unavailable.
- If a section finds nothing, write "Nothing notable." Don't pad.

### Signal preservation (don't flatten human analysis into unsourced consensus)
- For each major move, run one search aimed at named reaction, not just data — e.g. "strategist reaction 10-year Treasury 5% quote", "economist opinion stagflation odds named" — alongside the standard data query.
- Never write "analysts say" or "some believe." Name the person and their institution, or drop the claim.
- Quote directly (or closely paraphrase) when the specific wording carries the argument, not just the conclusion. Keep the reasoning chain a named source gives, not only their bottom line.
- When named sources disagree, present both sides with attribution — never merge into a false middle or an anonymous average.
- Link every attributed quote to its source.
- If a search result's byline looks stale or mismatched (e.g. a title that doesn't match other current reporting), flag it rather than silently repeating it as fact — treat it like a source disagreement.
- Keep three layers visibly separate: (1) fact — a sourced number or event, (2) reported analysis — a named person's attributed view, (3) "Read:" — Claude's own synthesis. A reader should always be able to tell which layer a sentence belongs to.

## Output (Markdown, all times Pacific)
1. **Top 3.** One line each: what happened and why it matters today.
2. **Dashboard.** Table: 2y, 10y, 30y, 2s10s, S&P futures, DXY, USD/JPY, WTI, gold, VIX, HY OAS. Columns: level, change vs yesterday, source, as-of time.
3. **What moved and why.** Rates, equities, FX, commodities, credit. Two to four sentences each.
4. **Playbook lens.** Read `playbook.md`. Tag stories to its themes (fiscal dominance, structural inflation, geopolitical fragmentation, private-credit liquidity, leverage/basis, passive concentration). Answer each Section 5 checklist question in one line, or "insufficient data".
5. **Today's calendar.** Data, auctions, Fed speakers, major earnings.
6. **Watch list.** What would change the read.
7. **Sources.** Numbered URLs.
