Brussels Makes the Energy-Crisis Warning Official, and Dates It to the Same Conflict

Geopolitical fragmentation

The European Commission sent EU member states a letter warning of a winter energy price crisis and urging gas-demand curbs, citing the ongoing Middle East conflict's spillover into energy markets. EU gas storage is ~70% full, about 12 points behind this time last year. Dutch TTF is trading near €72/MWh, up roughly €40 since the US and Israel struck Iran on Feb 28.

  • Energy Commissioner Dan Jørgensen: the region's dependence on imported fossil fuels exposes it to energy-market turmoil, and national governments need to urgently prepare for the heating season (Bloomberg, Sept 26).
  • Proposed measures echo 2022: reducing electricity consumption during peak hours, encouraging demand-shifting via smart meters and retail tariffs, limiting public-building temperatures, restricting outdoor heating, switching off unnecessary public lighting at night. Jørgensen stopped short of proposing mandatory measures.
  • Price level and driver: Dutch TTF near €72/MWh, up ~€40/MWh since the Feb 28 US/Israel strikes on Iran. Analysts cited in the reporting warn of spikes above €100/MWh during peak winter if Gulf LNG export volumes don't grow, or if Norwegian maintenance prolongs export constraints.
  • Storage gap: EU gas storage ~70% full, roughly 12 percentage points below the same point last year — a smaller cushion heading into the heating season than markets had going into the prior winter.

Bloomberg Names Bond-Market Volatility Itself as the Next Credit Signal

Private-credit liquidity

The MOVE index — Treasury volatility's equivalent of VIX — hit roughly 105 this week, the highest since March and well above its ~80 ten-year norm. Bloomberg's credit-weekly column frames that jump as a leading indicator for corporate-spread widening, comparable to early 2023. It hasn't shown up in spreads yet: IG was ~77bp Thursday, about 2bp tighter than the start of the month.

  • MOVE level: ~105 this week, “the most since March,” against a ~80 ten-year norm (Bloomberg credit-weekly, Sept 26). Historically, MOVE spikes like this have preceded corporate-spread widening, including in early 2023.
  • Spread levels haven't followed: US investment-grade spreads were ~77bp Thursday, about 2bp tighter than where they began the month — corporate bonds have been relatively resilient amid the broader government-bond selloff.
  • JPMorgan Chase strategists estimate US high-grade spreads are about 0.07 percentage point (7bp) too tight given the risk; MassMutual's Kelly Kowalski separately said corporate bonds are priced with little room for disappointment.
  • This sits alongside, not instead of, Saturday's Oracle-specific story: Oracle 2055 bonds ~77c, 5-year CDS at an all-time high, the ~$18B Project Jupiter construction loan below 90c. Oracle's underlying downgrade to BBB- by S&P (dated July 9 in company-level reporting) cited OpenAI's roughly half-share of Oracle's $638B remaining performance obligations and a widening FY2027 free-operating-cash-flow deficit toward $42B.
